Notes

Band neutrophil (stab cell): the last immature stage before the segmented neutrophil. Look for a curved, horseshoe- or C-shaped nucleus of fairly even width with no true constrictions into separate lobes. If the narrowest part of the nucleus is more than a third the width of the widest part, call it a band rather than a segmented cell. The chromatin is clumped but less condensed than in a mature neutrophil, and the cytoplasm is pale pink with fine specific granules. In this field the band sits just left of centre. The spiculated red cell below it is a burr cell (echinocyte) created during slide preparation rather than a true finding: the projections are short and evenly spaced, and the cell keeps its central pallor. Avoid reporting burr cells from thin, slowly dried areas of the smear. Bands normally make up about 0–5% of white cells in adult peripheral blood. A rise above that, often called a left shift, points toward infection or inflammation and should be read together with the total white count and the clinical history.
